How each option behaves

  • A (A1, A2, A3), corner platform. The hallway and back aisle wrap the back of the room, the entry and both restroom doors are behind the congregation, and the parking vestibule is the stage door. Fan seating uses the irregular floor well. A solid backdrop runs along the back of the platform and up its restroom-side end, with a 3 ft opening at the hallway end; the baby grand sits at the restroom-side end (house left); the corner behind the backdrop is storage or office space, about 125 SF, entered from the parking-lot vestibule; the pastor's office is the back corner at the top of the hallway, about 160 SF, with its door onto the back aisle. Classroom 1's corner at the lobby is cut at 45° so the turn into the hallway is open. Trade-off: a diagonal platform is less conventional to build and the sound/projection wall is a corner.
  • B, platform on the south. Straight rows and a straight platform in front of the Classroom 3 wall. The hallway still delivers people to the back-left corner and the restrooms are back-right, so the circulation rule holds. Trade-off: the office-row diagonal clips the back-left rows, the platform is only 10 ft deep unless Classroom 3 gives up the recess, and the parking vestibule lands beside the platform's right end (fine as a stage door, bad if people use it to come in).
  • C, platform on the west. Straight rows facing the hallway wall, restrooms on the back wall, and the hallway doubles as backstage for musicians. The problem is the front door: the hallway's north end opens beside the platform, so everyone walks in at the front-left. It also seats the fewest. Shown because you asked; not recommended unless the entry moves.

Things to measure or confirm on the next visit

  • Tape the hall: width between the 14 × 19 room and the right wall, and depth from the office row to the platform recess. Those two numbers set the seat count.
  • Locate both columns and note the ceiling height (looks like 9 ft tile grid) and whether office partitions stop at the ceiling tile or run to the deck.
  • Confirm the restroom block is exclusive to this suite. The broker's outline runs through the middle of it.
  • Remove the heavy-walled 12.6 × 8 room by the main entry so the lobby grows. If its walls turn out to be structural, or it is electrical or riser space, it stays and the lobby keeps its current size; Classroom 3 keeps its door from that room either way.
  • Ask the city about a Conditional Use Permit. Zoning is MP (Industrial Park); churches usually need a CUP there. A change of occupancy from B to A-3 also triggers a building permit. The building is sprinklered, so new walls mean relocating a few heads, not adding a system.
  • Parking: the building has 55 spaces shared with the leased 14,810 SF. Sunday use is your argument, but get it in writing.
